# Revisions Literature — Is the Problem Real? (Part B1)

**Research date: 2026-11-19.** Every paper below was verified via a page the researcher opened, unless marked *[search-only]*. Numbers are quoted only where actually seen in an abstract or full text; anything else is marked **unverified**. No paper, number or URL was invented.

**Headline case verified:** Singapore Q3 2025 manufacturing growth was **0.0% y/y in the 14 Oct 2025 advance estimate** and **~5.0% in the 21 Nov 2025 Economic Survey** — a +5 pp sectoral revision. Overall GDP: **2.9% → 4.2% y/y** (+1.3 pp). See §8.

## 1. Croushore & Stark (2001) — the real-time data foundation

**Citation:** Dean Croushore & Tom Stark, "A Real-Time Data Set for Macroeconomists," *Journal of Econometrics* 105(1), 111–130, Nov 2001. Working-paper version: Philadelphia Fed WP 99-4 (1999).

**Findings (abstract opened):** Introduces the Philadelphia Fed real-time vintage dataset. Re-examines key empirical macro papers and finds results are **not always robust across data vintages**: policy analysis and forecasts can change depending on which vintage is used. No mean-absolute-revision numbers visible in the abstract — **unverified**.

**Why it matters for Recall Press:** the academic canon already accepts that "the number" is not a single object but a sequence of vintages — yet every published document freezes one vintage forever.

**Links:** https://ideas.repec.org/p/fip/fedpwp/99-4.html *(opened)*; journal record https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/econom/v105y2001i1p111-130.html *[search-only]*; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304407601000720 *[search-only]*.

## 2. Stark & Croushore (2002) — forecasting with vintages

**Citation:** Tom Stark & Dean Croushore, "Forecasting with a Real-Time Data Set for Macroeconomists," *Journal of Macroeconomics* 24(4), 507–531, Dec 2002 (WP: Philadelphia Fed WP 01-10).

**Findings (abstract opened):** Data vintage matters for some forecasting issues but not others: it affects lag-length selection in univariate models; forecast-accuracy measures are vintage-sensitive on short (5-year) evaluation spans; crucially, **RMSE and MAE can be deceptively lower when using latest-available (revised) data instead of real-time data**. Related: Croushore & Stark (2003), "A Real-Time Data Set for Macroeconomists: Does the Data Vintage Matter?", *Review of Economics and Statistics* 85(3), 605–617 *[search-only]*. Exact revision-size numbers not seen — **unverified**.

**Link:** https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jmacro/v24y2002i4p507-531.html *(opened)*.

## 3. Aruoba (2008) — revisions are biased and predictable

**Citation:** S. Borağan Aruoba, "Data Revisions Are Not Well Behaved," *Journal of Money, Credit and Banking* 40(2–3), 319–340, Mar/Apr 2008.

**Findings (author's abstract opened):** Revisions to major US macro variables fail three desirable properties:
1. **Non-zero mean → initial announcements are biased.**
2. Revisions are **large relative to the original variables**.
3. Revisions are **predictable using information available at the initial announcement** → initial releases are not rational/efficient forecasts; they contain forecastable "noise," not just "news."

Exact magnitudes and sign-change frequencies are in the paper body, which could not be read (SSRN returned 403) — **numbers unverified**.

**Links:** https://boraganaruoba.com/research/data-revisions-are-not-well-behaved/ *(opened; free WP PDF linked there)*; https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=848607 *[search-only; fetch blocked]*; CEPR DP5271 https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/5271.html *[search-only]*.

## 5. OECD — revisions analysis infrastructure

**Verified citation:** Richard McKenzie (2006), "Undertaking Revisions and Real-Time Data Analysis using the OECD Main Economic Indicators Original Release Data and Revisions Database," *OECD Statistics Working Papers* 2006/02, DOI 10.1787/146528313656.

**Findings (OECD page opened):** First releases are often revised substantially; revisions can change the assessment of the economy and the output of econometric models; **most statistical agencies do not quantify expected revisions**; the OECD built the MEI revisions database so analysts can test models in simulated real time and agencies can study magnitude/direction of revisions. No country-level MAR numbers in the abstract — **unverified**.

**Link:** https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/undertaking-revisions-and-real-time-data-analysis-using-the-oecd-main-economic-indicators-original-release-data-and-revisions-database_146528313656.html *(opened)*.

## 6. IMF — G-20 GDP revisions around the 2008 crisis (numbers verified in full text)

**Citation:** Manik L. Shrestha & Marco Marini (2013), "Quarterly GDP Revisions in G-20 Countries: Evidence from the 2008 Financial Crisis," IMF Working Paper WP/13/60.

**Findings (full text opened on IMF eLibrary):** For y/y quarterly GDP growth, first estimate vs. estimate two years later:

| Country | Mean absolute revision, pre-2008 | Mean absolute revision, post-2008 |
|---|---|---|
| US | 0.53 pp | **1.13 pp** |
| UK | 0.24 pp | **0.95 pp** |
| Japan | 1.0 pp | 0.68 pp |
| South Africa | 0.84 pp | 0.38 pp |
| Turkey | 0.81 pp | 0.44 pp |

**Bias:** post-2008, **eight countries show negative mean revision** (first estimates systematically *overestimated* growth), vs. only four pre-2008. US mean revision post-2008 = **−1.13 pp** (all four revision horizons negative: −0.12, −0.13, −0.66, −1.13). UK mean revision ≈ 0 despite large offsetting revisions. Conclusion: crisis-period first estimates were less reliable and more often too optimistic.

**Link:** https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2013/060/article-A001-en.xml *(opened, full text)*.

## 7. The classics: Runkle (1998) and Mankiw & Shapiro (1986)

**Runkle:** David E. Runkle, "Revisionist History: How Data Revisions Distort Economic Policy Research," Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis *Quarterly Review* 22(4), Fall 1998, 3–12; DOI 10.21034/qr.2241. **Findings (abstract opened):** documents how/why US output-growth and inflation estimates are revised; concludes researchers must use **data available at the time of policy decisions**, not final revised data, to understand policymaker behaviour. Exact sizes in the PDF — **unverified**. Link: https://www.minneapolisfed.org/research/quarterly-review/revisionist-history-how-data-revisions-distort-economic-policy-research *(opened)*.

**Mankiw & Shapiro:** N. Gregory Mankiw & Matthew D. Shapiro, "News or Noise? An Analysis of GNP Revisions," NBER Working Paper 1939, June 1986; published in *Survey of Current Business* 66, 20–25, May 1986. **Findings (NBER abstract opened, numbers verified):** preliminary GNP errors are large — **if the preliminary estimate shows zero real GNP growth, one can be only 80% confident the final estimate (annual rate) lies between −2.8% and +2.8%** (±2.8 pp). But revisions are **not forecastable** from available information → preliminary estimates are efficient ("news," not predictable "noise"). Note this contrasts with Aruoba (2008), who finds predictability/bias in later samples — an honest tension in the literature. Link: https://www.nber.org/papers/w1939 *(opened)*.

## 8. Singapore — MTI advance-estimate methodology and the Q3 2025 case

**Methodology (opened):** MTI press-release footnote (26 Dec 2013, "Release of Advance GDP Estimates"): advance GDP estimates are "**computed largely from data in the first two months of the quarter**… intended as an early indication… **may be revised when more comprehensive data become available**"; only manufacturing, construction and services aggregates are published at advance stage. The same caveat appears in Oct 2025 release coverage (CNA, opened). Link: https://www.mti.gov.sg/newsroom/release-of-advance-gdp-estimates/ *(opened; redirects to meti.gov.sg — MTI's site migrated to meti.gov.sg on 1 Oct 2026)*.

**Gap:** **no published MAS/MTI/SingStat study quantifying advance-estimate accuracy** (mean revisions, hit rates) was found. Searches surfaced only press releases and news. This is a search gap, not evidence of absence.

**Q3 2025 headline case — VERIFIED:**

| Release | Date | Overall GDP y/y | Manufacturing y/y |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advance estimate | 14 Oct 2025 | **+2.9%** (+1.3% q/q SA) | **0.0% ("flat")**, slowing from Q2's 5.0% (+6.1% q/q SA) |
| Economic Survey 3Q2025 (final) | 21 Nov 2025 | **+4.2%** (+2.4% q/q SA) | **~5.0%** ("5 per cent", BT; +11.3% q/q SA) |

- Advance release (official page opened): https://www.meti.gov.sg/newsroom/singapore-gdp-grew-by-2-9-per-cent-in-third-quarter-of-2025/ — full PDF: https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/1004/c429212a-3d33-474e-b8fb-3e500069255e/AdvEst_3Q25.pdf. Corroborated by CNA, 14 Oct 2025 *(opened)*: https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/singapore-economy-grows-2-9-cent-third-quarter-2025-mti-advance-estimates-5399906
- Final release (official page opened): https://www.meti.gov.sg/newsroom/mti-upgrades-gdp-growth-forecast-for-2025-to--around-4-0-per-cent--and-forecasts-gdp-growth-of--1-0-to-3-0-per-cent--for-2026/ — full PDF: https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/1004/d90a2738-4f15-4452-a87d-949eba6a1c56/PR_3Q25.pdf *(linked, not parseable)*.
- Manufacturing final figure via Business Times, 21 Nov 2025 *(opened)*: "Manufacturing grew 5 per cent, down marginally from 5.1 per cent in the previous quarter… On a quarter-on-quarter, seasonally adjusted basis, manufacturing grew 11.3 per cent." https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/singapore/economy-policy/singapore-raises-2025-growth-forecast-to-around-4. The claimed "5.0%" matches BT's "5 per cent"; the exact one-decimal figure sits in the MTI PDF that could not be parsed — **verified via press reporting; decimal precision unverified**.
- CNBC (21 Nov, opened) confirms 4.2% vs 2.9% advance: https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/21/singapore-q3-gdp-forecast-mti-enterprise-exports-pharmaceuticals-drugs-tariffs-trade-mas.html
- MTI also upgraded the full-year 2025 forecast from "1.5–2.5%" to "around 4.0%" on 21 Nov. Full-year 2025 growth was later revised again, from 4.8% to 5.0% (Business Times, Feb 2026) *[search-only]*: https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/singapore/economy-policy/singapore-revises-2025-gdp-growth-5-4-8-earlier-bolstered-strong-q4

**Verdict:** the headline case is real — a **+5 pp sectoral revision** and **+1.3 pp headline revision** five weeks apart, with the official forecast range roughly doubling. Anyone who wrote "manufacturing is flat" on 14 October was wrong by 21 November; anyone who wrote "as first reported in October, manufacturing was flat" was still right. That distinction is Recall Press's core judgment.

## Synthesis

Four decades of research — Mankiw–Shapiro (1986) through Croushore–Stark (2001/2002), Aruoba (2008) and the IMF's G-20 study (2013) — agrees that first-release GDP figures carry substantial uncertainty: US advance-to-final mean absolute revision ≈ **1.2 pp** (BEA statistics via Econbrowser), an **80% interval of ±2.8 pp** around a zero preliminary print (Mankiw–Shapiro), and revisions that **grow larger around turning points and crises** (IMF: US MAR 0.53 → 1.13 pp post-2008). Later work finds early estimates are often **biased and predictable** rather than efficient (Aruoba; IMF finds eight G-20 countries systematically overestimated growth post-2008, US mean revision −1.13 pp). Revisions change forecast rankings, policy-rule estimates and real-time assessments of the economy (Croushore–Stark; Runkle). Singapore's Q3 2025 is a textbook live example: an advance estimate built on two months of data showed manufacturing flat and GDP at 2.9%; five weeks later manufacturing was up ~5% and GDP at 4.2%. **Honest caveats:** no published MAS/MTI accuracy study was found; Aruoba's and Fixler's exact tables remain unverified at source; Mankiw–Shapiro's "efficient revisions" conclusion conflicts with Aruoba's later findings — the literature disagrees about predictability, not about magnitude.
