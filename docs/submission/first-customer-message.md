# First customer · one pilot offer and three opening messages

## Pilot offer

- **Who it is for.** One editor of a recurring Singapore briefing, newsletter or board pack that quotes quarterly GDP or sector growth from the ten series Revisionpedia tracks.
- **What they send.** One slide deck, Word note or Markdown page they have already published and expect to reuse, and the date it was published. A public file is enough; nothing confidential.
- **What they get.** First, the list of sentences Recall Press linked, each with its series, quarter and dated source, plus any sentence it could not link. Then, after the next official release for those figures, either a note that nothing changed or a corrected editable copy with the reason, which the editor applies, skips or edits.
- **When.** The linked-sentence list within two working days of receiving the file. The release result on the day of the next scheduled release for those figures; the next GDP release is 24 November 2026 at 08:00 SGT.
- **Price.** None is set. Any fee for later editions is agreed with the editor before coverage continues.

There is no paying customer yet. The messages below open the conversation; the offer above is what the editor is asked to accept.

## Chamber or trade association editor

**Subject: Ten minutes with one member-briefing slide?**

I saw the quarterly GDP row in your Singapore economic data sheet. Do you have a member slide that uses it again? I’m building Recall Press so that when the figure changes, the editor gets the exact sentence and a corrected slide. Could we spend ten minutes before Tuesday’s release registering one public slide you expect to reuse? I’ll show you the source match and you can tell me whether it’s the right one.

## Research desk editor

**Subject: Which sentence should survive a GDP revision?**

Your July note recorded Singapore’s Q2 advance estimate at 5.7%. That’s still the right number for a sentence about July’s release. A current outlook slide might need 6.2%. Could we take ten minutes before Tuesday’s release to register one slide from a published briefing and check that distinction together? I’ll bring the two releases.

## Investor newsletter editor

**Subject: One slide for Tuesday’s GDP release**

Do you reuse an economy slide between issues? I’m testing Recall Press with editors ahead of Tuesday’s GDP release. Could we take ten minutes before the release to register one slide you’ve already made public? I’ll link its sentence to the official figure. After the release I’ll send you the proposed wording if the figure changes, or tell you it held.

## Ten Singapore publishing workflows

Public examples opened on 19 November 2026. The first two messages draw on the chamber sheet and bank note below. These pages establish that the organisations publish figures. Customer demand still needs a conversation.

1. **Chamber of commerce.** Quarterly member data sheets put GDP and trade figures side by side. [Economic Data of the Republic of Singapore](https://www.sgc.org.sg/en/content/download/149768/2418500?version=13)
2. **Bank economics desk.** Its July note records the 5.7% advance estimate for Q2 GDP. A slide reused later could need the revised figure. [DBS July research note](https://www.dbs.com.sg/private-banking/aics/archive/templatedata/article/generic/data/en/GR/072026/260714_insights_singapore.xml)
3. **Management consultancy.** The September quarterly review uses 5.9% for that same quarter. It makes a good dated comparison with the bank note. [Southeast Asia quarterly economic review](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/future-of-asia/southeast-asia-quarterly-economic-review)
4. **Business newsroom.** Quarterly release coverage records the estimate available on the day. A later explainer might use the figure again. [CNA coverage of the Q2 advance estimate](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore/gdp-singapore-economy-growth-mti-6250876)
5. **Independent equity research firm.** A recurring company update includes Singapore’s economic outlook alongside the company’s accounts. [FPA Financial rated update](https://api2.sgx.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/14.%2009102026_Rated%20Update_FPA_SBS%20Transit_Cycle%203.pdf)
6. **Property consultancy.** Quarterly market commentary interprets URA price changes. Ask first about a slide citing GDP, since property prices are outside the current archive. [JLL commentary on quarterly URA figures](https://www.jll.com/en-sea/newsroom/jll-commentary-on-ura-real-estate-statistics)
7. **Regional economic surveillance organisation.** Annual country consultations revisit Singapore’s growth and inflation history. [AMRO Singapore consultation series](https://www.amro-asia.org/member-economies/singapore/)
8. **Central bank.** A macroeconomic review cites the advance Q3 estimate and the revised Q2 growth rate together. [MAS review of the Singapore economy](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/publications/macroeconomic-review/2026/oct/mroct26_chpt_3.pdf)
9. **Economic ministry.** The economic survey puts sector and labour figures into narrative and chart form for repeated editions. [Economic Survey of Singapore](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/166/cb73b30d-6eaf-40bd-aa99-b1f12c4253c0/FullReport_AES2025.pdf)
10. **National statistical office.** Its newsletter explains how successive quarterly GDP estimates change as more data arrive. [Statistics Singapore Newsletter on GDP revisions](https://assets.app.optical.gov.sg/singstat/production/published/assets/2d99003e-c6f6-4dc4-ab5a-b77daa3b4af6.pdf)
