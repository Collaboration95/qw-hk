# Story: from context to a headline spine

Do this before any design. The spine is text only, cheap to change, and every variant deck shares it.

## 1. What are we optimising for?

Find these in the project before asking anything. Sources: `brief.md`, `hack/EVENT.md`, `hack/VIBE.md`,
`SPEC.md`, README, pasted judging criteria, organiser slides or transcripts, the running app.

| Must know | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Optimisation target**: what the judges or audience reward (brief, criteria, track, sponsor) | Sets technical depth and which slides exist. Common Grounds spent 6 of 12 slides on the Codex loop because the brief rewarded Codex use. Never put the prize itself in the story |
| **Slot**: minutes, finals or prelims, live demo or not, who presents | Slide count and script length |
| **Ethos**: the one-line desire behind the product | Slide 1 and the close argue it. Click asked for it after the deck was finished and slide 1 took 4 rounds |
| **The golden demo run**: one question or flow a non-expert understands | The deck mirrors its real values |
| **Audience**: how much of the domain they know | The plain-words test for slide 1 |
| **Must say / must not say**: sponsor names, local hooks, claims to avoid | Copy constraints |

If any of the first three is not visible, ask once, early, in one batch: at most 6 numbered questions, each
with lettered options and one marked recommended, so "1A 2C 3B" is a full answer. Draft the ethos for them:
offer 2–3 candidate lines and a recommendation. Ask nothing that the files answer. Never ask a second batch
before showing decks.

## 2. Claims inventory

List every fact the deck might state, with its source (file, run log, URL). Drop anything you cannot trace or
the presenter could not explain in one breath. Mark simulated or synthetic data. This list feeds the fact check
and the appendix.

## 3. The arc (default for a hackathon pitch)

| # | Job | Default headline pattern | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Brand moment, said while greeting | (empty, then the logo reveal) | brand tile + wordmark |
| 1 | The felt, two-sided problem + what it is | "[Shift in the world]. [The tension]." + one-line answer | arrow rows, one wall/blocker, marker on the felt word |
| 2 | The idea in one picture | "Same simple text box. A new screen every time." | the product's signature visual |
| 3–5 | The demo: one slide per flow (max 3) or a demo map of the golden run | the user's own words in quotes, or "One question, start to finish." | real screens in frames, the mechanism as chips and steps, one real number |
| 6 | Proof or progress | "8 taps moved Guru from 7.9% to 43.4%." / "What we built since [milestone]." | before → after; photo card for external validation |
| 7 | How it works, ideal | "The LLM calls tools to render the UI." | role pipeline |
| 8 | Why it works / reach | "Why it works." / "Any agent, by URL." | three word-labelled cards / logos + one URL |
| 9 | Close | "What's next." (two items) or the tagline bookend | big QR (≥ 420 px), "Check it out", brand tile |
| A | Appendix | "Appendix" | numbered Q&A tiles |

Each number appears on one slide only; the spine must not repeat a figure across slides (the idea slide shows
the picture, the proof slide shows the number).

Adjust to the optimisation target: a technical prize earns 2–4 mechanism slides (real code, logs, contracts);
a consumer or business brief earns flows and one number. Drop slides rather than shrink type.

**Spoken path**: write which slides are spoken and which are fallback (the demo map and deep explainers often
are). The script follows the spoken path.

## 4. Copy rules, with real before → after

| Rejected | Approved |
|---|---|
| "Shopping apps are one of two screens." | "Shopping apps box you in. AI opens it up." |
| "Shopping apps show everything. AI apps show nothing." | (same as above: "it's not a problem that the AI apps show nothing") |
| "The LLM calls tools. It never writes UI code." | "The LLM calls tools to render the UI." |
| "Honest scope · What is real, what is synthetic, what is next." | cut; caveats spoken |
| "Assumptions · What we are betting on." (5 bets, 123 words) | "Why it works." Frictionless: "Know what you want? Two taps." / Discovery: "A new screen is a reason to look." / Insight: "Fun in, insight out." |
| "ResearchAgent lets an agent buy the one article it needs, inside your budget, and pays the writer the moment it unlocks." | "ResearchAgent: agents buy the article they need, writers get paid." |
| "What you'll see." | "One question, start to finish." |
| "Paid on delivery. Proven on the ledger." (tagline) | removed |
| "Status and limits: No outside customers, pilots or revenue…" | "What's next." + two items |
| "Next: real money with a licensed payments partner…" | "Licensed publishers selling to agents" · "Stablecoin (RLUSD) settlement" |
| "347 merged pull requests · 15 releases · 54 days" | removed; before/after only |
| "Server: Holds the API key." / "Logistic regression, ~190 features." | "Server: Brings the user's context." / "Preference engine: Learns what each shopper likes." |
| "Shopee, Amazon, Coupang" caption | removed (no competitor names) |
| numbered cards "1 2 3" | word labels: "Frictionless", "Discovery", "Insight", ordered by persuasion |

- Copy in the product's own voice is fine, even playful (Prompt Fu: "Glazed and confused."). Generic filler is not.
- Local hooks for the room earn a place (Click's Korean tagline for Korean judges), always with romanisation
  and meaning, on the title and the close.
- When asked for copy, give a recommended line plus one alternative per point.
