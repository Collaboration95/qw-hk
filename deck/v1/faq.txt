Appendix keys: 9 then 1-6 open the cards.

1. Has anyone paid for coverage after seeing their own slide corrected?
   Not yet. The founder wrote the sample publications. The first proof would be an editor paying to cover a second edition after seeing their own slide corrected.

2. Can you open a QwenWork task now and show which part the agent did?
   Not yet verified. The plan: pick the Skill in a fresh task, watch it read the briefing, give its reasons and deliver slide four. Today the local replay shows what the scripts do.

3. Did the clock run twice without the founder clicking?
   Not yet. Two time-triggered runs must show the same project and their records. On unchanged data the second run should issue no duplicate correction.

4. Can the agent find a claim your rules missed on a different deck?
   Not yet. The rules found three of four verifiable claims on one unseen sample deck; the agent must read for the fourth. (Appendix 1: 40 unseen claims, 28 right, 9 handed to the agent with a reason, 3 not surfaced, 0 confidently wrong.)

5. Can I find that figure on a public page and trace its enquiry into CRM?
   Not yet. The record page and its enquiry form exist in the Revisionpedia build; public hosting and a test enquiry reaching CRM still need checking.

6. Why buy this if charts already refresh in PowerPoint?
   A refreshed chart can sit under a headline that still says "stalled". We find that sentence across registered briefings and hand the editor replacement wording and an editable file.

7. What stops the agent from silently changing a board minute?
   Each claim records whether it describes today or a dated release. October really did report flat output, so the minute keeps that statement; only the current headline gets a proposed replacement, with a reason. (Appendix 2.)

8. What about the copy a member downloaded yesterday?
   It stays on their device. The editor can send the corrected copy and cite the release behind it. We cannot recall a download.

9. Does an email start a background job, and can the sender dictate the new value?
   The planned route starts when the editor selects a forwarded notice and asks the task to check it. The check uses the official series, basis and quarter, never the sender's number.

10. Does 57 of 60 mean almost every slide needs fixing?
    No. The 60 are series-quarters with more than one reading in our archive; 57 changed at least once, 21 crossed zero or an illustrative cutoff at some point. Whether a slide needs fixing depends on its wording.

11. Why trust the agent with an ambiguous sentence or a failed source check?
    An unresolved claim is held with a reason, and a failed source check is reported. The editor applies, skips or edits each proposal.

12. Why would anyone visit Revisionpedia before they know you?
    A reader can look up why Q3 manufacturing is quoted as 0.0%, 5.0% and 4.6% and follow the source for each reading. Search and AI-citation traffic still need measuring.

13. Can the public directory expose a subscriber's deck?
    No. The directory is built from statistical records with links to dated releases. Only the founder's labelled samples belong on a public demo page.

14. Can one founder deliver this profitably?
    Unproven. We would measure the editor's manual correction time against registration, agent cost, review time and exceptions over several releases, and compare the cost per briefing with what an editor actually pays.

15. What if the 24 November release changes nothing?
    We show the actual run record: when the data was checked and whether it succeeded. An unchanged figure gets a quiet result; a failed fetch gets a visible failure. The November 2025 example stays a dated historical replay.

16. Is anyone still quoting old numbers?
    Yes: a claim-specific screen found 2 live web pages still quoting superseded figures, 6 and 57 days after the revision. It is a screen, not a census. (Appendix 3.)

17. Where does the data come from?
    SingStat tables via data.gov.sg under the Singapore Open Data Licence v1.0; dated MTI and SingStat releases for earlier readings. (Appendix 4.)

18. Who pays?
    Recurring briefing editors, for coverage per publication. Price is not yet validated. (Appendix 6.)
